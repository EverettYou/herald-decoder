## Summary

An exact GF(2) quadratic-phase sum replaces enumeration of affine first-projector subsets for the same physical projector instrument. It replays all 55 previously computed selected-first-record masses and all 2,420 single-site expectations from 110 existing complete second laws. This validates a method prerequisite, not a transfer symmetry between private errors and not a new full-prior result.

## Evidence

The [prospective contract](../../manifests/j9e-quadratic-character-quotient-2026-09-28.json) freezes the selected L=2 D4 channel, the two relation-free public actions, four machine inputs, 65,536 signed-phase evaluations and 60 CPU seconds. The [machine result](../../results/j9e-quadratic-character-quotient-2026-09-28.json) passes 150 exhaustive synthetic width-0–3 quadratic forms, 4,727 held-out affine-phase checks, 55 stored first-mass replays, 2,420 stored second-site expectation replays, and 194 noncommuting-zero controls. It uses 9,453 signed-phase evaluations in 0.473167 CPU seconds, with no new complete public law, history, schedule arm or bootstrap.

The fixed-prefix cost stress examined only the first 256 same-flux error masks from the J8J coset. Its deterministic maximum-variable-site fixture has 19 variable first sites but affine-kernel dimension three, and its diagnostic all-plus first mass is `1/65536`. This value is not promoted as a new public-law result. The 55 previously computed controls have affine-kernel dimensions at most two; their replay does not demonstrate an asymptotic speed advantage over the earlier small-k signed-affine solver.

## Status

The registered algebra/replay gate passes. A subsequent separately registered selected-first-record census could test whether all 8,192 same-flux private errors fit the inherited work cap; this gate does not assert that they do. Other first records, second complete laws, full-prior mixtures, logical risk, noisy JIT and threshold remain open. The quotient is a way to evaluate a character sum, not a stochastic/public-output map that licenses law pooling.
