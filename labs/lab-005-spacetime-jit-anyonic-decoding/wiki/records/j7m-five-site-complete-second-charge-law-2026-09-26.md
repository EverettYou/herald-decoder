## Summary

For one fixed three-red-edge physical error and one selected positive
complete first public record on the periodic L=2 ideal projector model,
two public corrections have the same full second flux but different
complete second charge laws. The exact full-public total-variation
distance is `15/16`. This is a finite ideal action effect on a charge
record, not evidence of logical-risk or JIT-schedule benefit.

## Evidence

The [prospective distinct contract](../../manifests/j7m-five-site-complete-second-charge-law-2026-09-26.json)
freezes the original J7K baseline `[0]` and trivial-loop-toggled
`[1,30,32,34,35]` public red-X corrections, physical error `[0,4,3]`,
selected signal-only full-binary first record of mass `1/4`, the same
anyon-free orbit and geometry, and the unchanged complete 24-site
second-record schema. J7L found that no six-edge loop fits its earlier
three-site cap. A *new* five-variable-site exact-law bound was justified
prospectively by an upper estimate of 16,384 ordered moments for 32
joint loop outcomes, below the earlier 65,536-term screen ceiling; it
does not retroactively alter J7K or J7L.

The [exact machine result](../../results/j7m-five-site-complete-second-charge-law-2026-09-26.json)
contains both normalized full public second laws and all positive
outcomes. Baseline has two charge strings, each probability `1/2`;
the loop toggle has 32 strings, each `1/32`. Both public second flux
arrays are identical, and the two baseline charge strings are within
the loop law's support. Consequently the exact full-record
total-variation distance is `15/16`, due solely to charge bits (and
their vacuum complements), not public flux. All J7K one-site
marginals replay, ten source/result/caller pins hold before and after,
two focused tests pass, and the calculation uses 17,868 of 32,768
registered ordered moment terms in about 0.05 CPU seconds. There are
zero next-first laws, stochastic histories, schedule arms, bootstrap
replicates or five-round caller edits.

## Status

This establishes that the ideal action choice changes the measured
second charge distribution on this selected fixture, without hiding
the difference in an already distinct public flux. It does *not*
measure mutual information or Bayesian risk about the private error,
show that the frozen JIT policy emits this long action, establish a
future-round feedback law, or support a noisy fault-tolerance claim.
The next dependent exact question is whether a same-future-key
next-first charge-law comparison is computationally and physically
well defined under a separate prospective bound.
