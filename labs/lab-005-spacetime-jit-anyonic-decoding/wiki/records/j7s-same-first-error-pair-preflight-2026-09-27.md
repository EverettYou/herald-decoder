## Summary

All twelve previously frozen shortest trivial-loop toggles of the
selected private initial error can produce the *same* complete first
public record with positive exact ideal probability. This provides a
finite two-error information-test prerequisite, not an information
measurement. The selected lexicographic pair is extremely imbalanced
under the declared sparse IID prior after first-record conditioning.

## Evidence

The [prospective contract](../../manifests/j7s-same-first-error-pair-preflight-2026-09-27.json)
freezes the 12-loop matrix, paper-normalized L=2 geometry, J7M full
first record, private base red-X error `[0,4,3]`, IID `p_X=1/10`, and
the lexicographically first positive-candidate rule. The
[machine result](../../results/j7s-same-first-error-pair-preflight-2026-09-27.json)
passes nine source/result hashes before and after, replays base first
mass `1/4`, and checks all twelve candidates. Every candidate has the
same first public flux and positive exact mass for the *full* binary
charge/vacuum record. The calculation used 1,956 ordered moments, well
under 16,384, and 0.013 CPU seconds, under 60. No second law, history,
schedule arm or bootstrap was evaluated.

The registered rule selects candidate initial error
`[1,3,4,30,32,34,35]`, obtained by toggling cycle
`[0,1,30,32,34,35]`. Its selected first-record mass is `1/64`.
Restricting the IID prior to only this base/candidate pair *and then*
conditioning on the selected first record gives candidate weight
`1/104977`. The binary entropy of this pair is about `0.000173` bit,
an upper bound on any additional information about its binary error
identity from later records at this chosen sparse rate. This is not
the posterior, entropy or risk of the complete IID error ensemble.

## Status

The first-record positivity prerequisite passes, but a complete
action-conditioned second public law for the alternative error has
not been computed; private-error information remains unmeasured.
The tiny entropy ceiling makes this lexicographic sparse-prior pair
a weak practical discriminator even if its later laws differ. Before
spending exact-law terms, the next bounded design should compare
the already screened candidate weights and cost ceilings under a
prospectively declared rule, or explicitly keep this rare pair as a
structural control. Any unconditional logical-risk study still needs
its separate full-channel denominator and paired private scorer.
