## Summary

The frozen ideal L=2 red-X channel's first-flux sectors reached by at most three physical errors carry `51.2938052191612%` of the exact IID prior, up from `28.8680551769476%` for at most two. A complete all-zero-charge *base-error* first law for each newly reached sector is projected to require 9,360 exact terms, below the inherited 65,536-term ceiling. No new Born law was evaluated in this prerequisite.

## Evidence

The [prospective contract](../../manifests/j8t-weight-three-prior-and-law-cost-2026-09-28.json) fixes the remediated D4 ideal public interface, 36 IID red-X edges at `p_X=1/10`, the pre-action full-binary first flux and all-zero-charge candidate record, and two independent branches: exact sector-prior coverage and base-first-law cost. The [machine-readable result](../../results/j8t-weight-three-prior-and-law-cost-2026-09-28.json) enumerates all 7,140 three-edge supports. They add 7,020 distinct first-flux sectors (120 support collisions, no overlap with the 667 lower-weight sectors), for 7,687 reached sectors and exact cumulative prior `0.512938052191612`. The complementary unreached prior is `0.487061947808388`.

For one lexicographically selected three-edge base per new sector, full first-star eligibility and pairwise commutation pass. There are 4,896 bases with zero variable sites, 2,016 with one and 108 with two; projected complete all-zero-charge base-first costs sum to `4,896 + 2×2,016 + 4×108 = 9,360` terms. Exact prior summation used 57,507,840 integer-weight evaluations and 8.536158 CPU seconds, below the registered limits. Born terms, first masses, second laws, histories, schedule arms and bootstraps were zero. Prior equality alone is not a geometry or operator-law symmetry.

## Status

Both preregistered branches pass. This is an explicit green light for a *separately registered* new-base first-law census, not permission to infer missing private-error laws or channel-wide information/risk. Nearly half the channel prior remains in first-flux sectors unreached by weight-at-most-three errors, and action-conditioned second charge laws remain unevaluated for these new sectors. No noisy JIT or threshold inference follows.
