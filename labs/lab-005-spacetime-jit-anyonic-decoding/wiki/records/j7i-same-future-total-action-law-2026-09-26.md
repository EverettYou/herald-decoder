## Summary

In one selected ideal three-edge case, changing the public correction while
holding both the initial physical error and the later physical fault fixed
changes the complete next-first public record with exact total variation
one. This finite *total action effect* is entirely separated by the public
flux component: it does not establish extra information from fusion charge,
better logical correction, or a noisy just-in-time schedule benefit.

## Evidence

The [preregistered four-cell contract](../../manifests/j7i-same-future-total-action-law-2026-09-26.json)
uses the same preselected positive complete first record (mass `1/4`),
initial red-X support, vacuum orbit, and projector semantics as the
[earlier matched-final-support history contrast](j7g-three-edge-matched-complete-next-law-2026-09-25.md).
It crosses two public corrections with two private later fault keys, and
compares actions only within a shared future-key column. Unlike that
earlier contrast, it does not condition on the post-action final support.

The [exact machine result](../../results/j7i-same-future-total-action-law-2026-09-26.json)
enumerates and normalizes the complete action-conditioned second public
law and subsequent full 24-site next-first law in every cell. For later
fault edge `0`, the two corrections end on supports `{0,3,4}` and `{3}`;
for later fault edge `4`, they end on `{3}` and `{0,3,4}`. In both columns,
the next public flux arrays differ and hence the complete-public supports
are disjoint: exact total variation is `1` in each column. This is a
deterministic flux distinction; the charge law need not be invoked to
separate them. Both previously computed diagonal complete laws replay
exactly. Seven immutable pins, five negative binding controls, two focused
tests and exact replay pass. The run used 15,724 ordered-moment terms,
well below the registered 262,144 cap, and no stochastic histories,
schedule evaluations or bootstrap replicates.

## Status

The result closes one finite ideal same-future total-action question.
It does not contradict the earlier exact `1/2` same-final-support history
contrast, because that comparison changes the future physical fault.
Nor is `TV=1` evidence that charge observations improve decoding:
the already-public flux component distinguishes the final sectors. The
physical five-round caller remains unchanged and unvalidated for this
stateful next-first transition. A further study would need a separately
registered estimand that removes this trivial flux distinction if the
scientific target is charge-specific information or a matched risk benefit.
