## Summary

Every one of six unequal second-public-law pair/action cells in the frozen deterministic-first class has a different affine parity rank and therefore a different positive-support cardinality between its two private errors. No invertible affine transformation of the shared public charge bits, including sign flips or site permutations, can carry one paired law to the other. This is a necessary-condition obstruction in an evaluated finite class, not a general operator no-go.

## Evidence

The [prospective contract](../../manifests/j9d-affine-relabeling-gate-2026-09-28.json) pins the [earlier exact 48-law character census](../../results/j9b-cross-sector-character-census-2026-09-28.json) and tests all 12 error pairs under both unchanged public actions. The [machine gate](../../results/j9d-affine-relabeling-gate-2026-09-28.json) verifies for every law that the number of positive active-bit strings is `2^(n-r)` for `n` active bits and parity rank `r`. Its 18 equal-law controls have equal rank; all six positive-total-variation cells have ranks `(1,0)`, `(2,0)` or `(0,2)`, hence unequal support sizes. Execution took 0.002832 CPU seconds and added zero operator characters, Born terms, public laws, histories, schedule arms or bootstrap draws.

## Status

This closes only bijective affine public-output relabeling between the paired laws in the frozen deterministic-first class. It does not exclude non-bijective signed-character identities, a reduction of other first-record sectors, or a mixture computation under the full physical prior. No full-IID information/risk, noisy just-in-time performance, scaling or threshold is inferred. The next bounded design question is whether a non-bijective relation can be specified and falsified without exceeding the inherited work cap; otherwise the program needs a scope/resource choice, not an invented law transfer.
