## Summary

At one selected complete first record on the frozen ideal periodic D4
orbit, all twelve alternative physical errors have the same exact
second-charge singleton and pair parity moments as the base under both
fixed public actions. All 208 tested moments, including base controls,
are zero. This resolves the prior one-site `unresolved` labels as fair
but does not establish equality of the five-bit joint record.

## Evidence

The [preregistered contract](../../manifests/j7y-low-order-second-moments-2026-09-27.json)
crosses twelve same-first-record errors with both actions and adds two
base controls. Its second sites are `[0]` under the first action and
`[0,17,20,21,22]` under the second. It tests their six total
singleton and ten second-action pair parity means per physical error.
All other eligible sites were already algebraically proven
deterministic-plus by the preceding [one-site screen](j7x-all-error-operator-discriminator-2026-09-27.md).

For a selected-first projector `P` and a second-site operator product
`B`, anticommutation with any first projector makes `PBP=0`. Otherwise
the conditional mean is the ratio of exact orbit-moment sums
`sum_S <A_S B> / sum_S <A_S>`. The [machine result](../../results/j7y-low-order-second-moments-2026-09-27.json)
passes input pins, exact first-mass and public-flux replay,
commutation, previous resolved one-site labels, and the base/two-peer
complete-law controls. Across 24 candidate/action cells plus two base
cells, every one of 208 singleton/pair moments equals `0`; none
distinguishes a candidate from the base. The calculation used 14,252
exact orbit-moment terms, below its 65,536 cap, and zero ordered
projector expansions, histories, policy arms or bootstrap replicates.

## Status

This closes the bounded order-one/two discriminator on the selected
record and geometry. In particular all five variable loop-action
charges are individually fair and pairwise uncorrelated for all
thirteen tested physical errors. Pairwise independence does not imply
five-bit joint independence: order-three, four or five correlations
could still distinguish complete second laws. No full-IID
information, unconditional logical risk or noisy JIT conclusion
follows. The next prospective prerequisite is a bounded higher-order
joint-moment matrix on the same fixed error/action set.

The later [complete higher-order calculation](j7z-higher-order-second-law-2026-09-27.md)
finds those remaining three-to-five-site moments zero too, and
establishes the complete-law equality for these thirteen errors on
this selected first-record slice. That later result does not
generalize to the full IID error support or noisy JIT policy.
