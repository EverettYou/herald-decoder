## Analysis

### L005.6 Implications

The Lab can advance through interface tests while retaining a clear separation between structural translation and performance evidence. An odd measured snapshot now reaches the spatial-policy boundary as an explicit causal temporal defer ledger rather than an invented spatial correction. The integrated caller can later supersede it with a bound full-snapshot even action in the public record and private frame score. The disjoint finite pilot shows why schedule claims still need stronger evidence: herald access helps one clean-readout contrast but harms the corresponding noisy-readout contrast, and JIT is worse than an online baseline in two triggered comparisons. Frozen outcome accounting shows that these net differences are mostly in scored failures, so resolving defers alone is unlikely to explain them. Full-cohort replay finds that late physical change and noisy readout often co-occur; neither flag is established as a cause. See [spatial policy boundary](wiki/spatial-policy-boundary.md) and [schedule state](wiki/schedule-state.md).

### L005.7 Limitations

The frozen J6 production run is complete but non-informative. It retained all 272 independent histories and 2,176 arm rows. The old static periodic inner decoder rejected valid odd instantaneous noisy syndromes, producing 960/1,024 failed-closed rows in the clean-herald cell and 944/1,024 in the joint-readout cell; these remain unconditional failures rather than disappearing. Zero control passed and the joint-readout-noise cell cleared the event gate, but clean-herald JIT invoked on only seven histories per mode, one short of the preregistered minimum. Its stop rule censors every J6 schedule-risk and mode-effect interpretation and prohibits bootstrap or added histories. In the disjoint J6B replacement, all gates passed, but 922 stochastic rows still failed closed and arm risks nearly saturated. The 14 paired intervals are pointwise, not multiplicity-adjusted, and the four triggered directions conflict. More fundamentally, its exogenous future hidden state is unchanged by an earlier action, so its finite timing comparisons cannot establish the non-Abelian hiding/revelation mechanism. Neither J6 nor J6B establishes a fault-tolerance threshold or universal JIT advantage. See [schedule state](wiki/schedule-state.md).
