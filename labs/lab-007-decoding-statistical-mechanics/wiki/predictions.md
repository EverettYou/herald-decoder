# Discriminating intrinsic, BKT-like, and algorithmic explanations

## Summary

The model supplies decoder-independent observables and separates what is now
exact from what requires a continuum argument or a future finite-size study.
The next scientific target is the typical-record sector distribution. A
restricted all-singlet loop ensemble and practical LER merging do not determine it.

## Evidence

### Predictions supported by the derivation

1. **SU(2) orientation control.** Exact Z_h, Bayes risk, and edge marginals must
   agree under directed and hidden channels for every graph and prior. A violation
   falsifies the implementation or changes the local instrument. Equal BP stopping
   iterations require matching its convergence metric, not merely the quotient.
2. **Non-Abelian current obstruction.** A plain divergence-free integer-current
   model cannot reproduce trivalent SU(3) odd-syndrome singlet records without
   additional sources. Conversely, U(1) must pass exact current and height
   reconstruction. Both statements are tested on the registered finite motifs.
3. **Independent hardening loss.** Better BP marginals need not remove the loss
   of sector information from choosing a marginal-weighted chain. The measured
   finite counterexamples reject that equivalence even on a factor tree.
4. **No generic N fugacity.** On the specified trivalent singlet record, the
   derived fugacity is 1 for SU(2) and 2 for hidden complex fundamentals. A
   proposed N-dependent loop gas must first reproduce these microscopic weights.

These predictions follow from [conjugacy](conjugacy.md), the
[statistical model](statistical-model.md), and the [decoder counterexamples](decoder-gap.md),
not from fits to the inherited performance curves.

### Observable that directly answers recoverability

For each observed record compute Q_h, ΔF=ln(Z₀/Z₁),
r*=1/(1+exp|ΔF|), and binary logical entropy
S_log=−∑_h Q_h ln Q_h. Average with the **physical** record distribution.
The ensemble is recoverable in the stated binary score if E[r*]→0. For one
logical bit this is equivalent to |ΔF|→∞ in probability: bounded convergence
proves one direction, while E[r*] bounds the probability of bounded |ΔF| for
the other. This criterion alone assigns no critical universality.

Track the distribution and tails of |ΔF| as well as its mean. A few divergent
records or a postselected all-singlet sample cannot certify typical inference.
The correct sector free-energy contrast is a quenched observable; replacing
E[min_h Z_h/Z] with a ratio of averaged partition functions is invalid.

### Competing explanations and tests

| Explanation | Discriminating evidence | What would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional finite-threshold transition | Typical-record Bayes-risk/sector-entropy scaling consistent with a finite correlation length away from one critical point; stable model comparison as sizes grow | Only the practical decoder changes, or finite-size drift prevents a stable intrinsic scale |
| BKT-like current/height transition | First derive the typical-record compact effective action and allowed defects; then compare essential correlation-length scaling and stiffness/winding diagnostics with conventional alternatives | The required continuous current/height symmetry is absent, sources are relevant, or the effect vanishes in exact sector inference |
| Algorithmic crossover or transition | Exact sector risk separates from exact-marginal and BP-marginal matching on identical records; the practical feature responds to BP controls | The same feature persists in controlled sector inference while algorithmic gaps shrink |
| Smooth crossover or critical interval | Neither single-point scaling description predicts held-out sizes; intrinsic distributions change smoothly or retain scale dependence over a range | Controlled intrinsic data select a single transition with stable predictive behavior |

The alternatives can coexist. Fusion-informed optimal decoding can improve
while practical BP-matching becomes worse. The [source comparison](source-context.md)
also rules out borrowing an integer-rotor stiffness jump as a prediction for
our open-boundary, parity-scored problem.

### Concrete follow-on study design, not acquired data

Keep the parent Bernoulli channel, shared signs, full interior record, open
rough boundaries, and residual binary score fixed. Construct an exact
transfer contraction on narrow square and honeycomb strips, carrying a
logical-cut parity bit along with frontier edge states. Retain the positive
local tensors of the present model, rather than a character integral with
cancellations. Validate against all current finite fixtures before increasing
width. Exact contraction at fixed width is a controlled finite-width oracle;
extrapolation across widths is a distinct task.

For every physical record, compare (i) sector Bayes, (ii) exact-marginal chain
matching, and (iii) the frozen parent BP-matching schedule. Report full-record
versus m-only Bayes risk within the same physical channel; compare orientation
channels separately because their record laws differ. Include BP residuals
and convergence without treating nonconvergence as logical failure. Any
sampled study must preregister sizes, record sampling and uncertainty before
production. This launch has acquired **no new Monte Carlo curves**.

A torus winding/helicity experiment would be a new geometry and score adapter.
Before it can support BKT claims, specify integer winding sectors and how their
parity projects to the binary code. No universal jump, p_c, or stiffness value
has been fitted here. The next theory question is whether typical SU(3) irrep
sources permit a controlled continuum description, or instead require a
representation-resolved vertex theory throughout.

## Status

Falsifiable follow-on predictions and observables established; continuum
universality and thermodynamic recovery remain open. The original bounded
formulation contract does not require running the proposed strip study.
