# Integer Linear Programming Decoding

**Summary**: Integer linear programming (ILP) can decode topological orders with general, correlated, or non-Abelian fusion rules by treating physical errors, readout errors, and allowed fusion channels as constrained discrete variables.

**Sources**: [Integer Linear Programming Decoder for Abelian and Non-Abelian Topological Codes](/reference?id=jing2026-ilp-topological-decoder)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-28

## Why matching is insufficient in general

MWPM assumes a pairwise reduction of the syndrome. General topological orders can have multiple anyon species, correlated local creation events, and fusion networks that branch or terminate on other strings. In non-Abelian orders, fusion outcomes can also be probabilistic. Jing *et al.* encode these possibilities as linear constraints rather than forcing a pairwise matching representation. [Jing *et al.* (2026), §§I–II](/reference?id=jing2026-ilp-topological-decoder)

## ILP formulation

The discrete decision vector contains physical-error variables, a selected local fusion channel at each syndrome site, and—when readout is faulty—measurement-error variables. The optimizer seeks the maximum-weight configuration subject to fusion-multiplicity constraints:

\[
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in\mathbb Z^n} w^\mathsf{T}x.
\]

The weights encode the stated local error model and the probability of a measured fusion outcome. This gives a minimum-weight approximation to the posterior configuration, not a sum over all configurations in a logical class; it should therefore not be called logical maximum-likelihood decoding. [Jing *et al.* (2026), §§II, V, and VII](/reference?id=jing2026-ilp-topological-decoder)

## Scope and computational boundary

The paper reports threshold studies for (mathbb Z_2) depolarizing noise, (mathbb Z_3), and (D_4) with all anyon species generated by incoherent noise. The ILP runtime has a broad instance-dependent tail; the reported accessible subthreshold (mathbb Z_2) data are consistent with polynomial scaling, but this does not remove the method's worst-case hardness or establish a real-time bound. [Jing *et al.* (2026), §§III–VI and Appendix A](/reference?id=jing2026-ilp-topological-decoder)

The method is a useful small-to-medium-system oracle and a way to retain fusion constraints that a matching graph discards. Whether it is a deployable back end for a future Herald Decoder depends on the eventual spacetime model, solver latency, and approximation strategy.
