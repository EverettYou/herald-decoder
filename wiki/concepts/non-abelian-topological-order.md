# Non-Abelian Topological Order

**Summary**: Non-Abelian topological order has anyons with multi-channel fusion and noncommuting braid actions. Fusion outcomes can be observable syndrome information, but their availability and noise model must be specified before using them for decoding.

**Sources**: [Symmetry Fractionalization, Defects, and Gauging of Topological Phases](/reference?id=barkeshli2014-symmetry-fractionalization); [Non-Abelian Topological Order and Anyons on a Trapped-Ion Processor](/reference?id=iqbal2023-nonabelian-topological-order); [Intrinsic Heralding and Optimal Decoders for Non-Abelian Topological Order](/reference?id=jing2025-intrinsic-heralding); [Fermionic Magnons and Non-Abelian Spinons](/reference?id=yao2011-su2-kitaev); [Stability and Loop Models from Decohering Non-Abelian Topological Order](/reference?id=sala2025-decohering-nonabelian-topological-order)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-04

## Fusion channels and decoder records

For a non-Abelian anyon (a), fusion need not have a unique outcome:

\[
a \times \bar a = 1 + b + \cdots.
\]

The unresolved channel is an internal degree of freedom, while a measured fusion outcome is a classical record. Jing *et al.* show in their fixed-point (D_4 \cong \mathbb Z_4 \rtimes \mathbb Z_2) model that finite-depth physical error strings leave intermediate fusion outcomes along their paths. Measuring those outcomes can reveal information about the error string without auxiliary flag qubits. [Jing *et al.* (2025), pp. 1–3](/reference?id=jing2025-intrinsic-heralding)

This is a scientifically useful instance of a herald: it is not a known erased edge and it is not equivalent to an ordinary group-representation label. It is a syndrome component generated by non-deterministic anyon fusion. The correspondence to the project's proposed fusion-remnant witness is an analogy in observation structure, not yet an identified microscopic realization.

## The \(D_4\) quantum double as a concrete example

The (D_4) topological order is a quantum-double model with non-Abelian charges. The decoder analysis focuses on a two-dimensional non-Abelian charge whose self-fusion has four Abelian channels:

\[
[2] \times [2] = 1 + s_1 + s_2 + s_3.
\]

An adaptive 27-qubit kagome-lattice circuit on a trapped-ion processor prepared a (D_4) topological-order state, demonstrated non-Abelian interferometry through Borromean-ring spacetime braiding, and accessed its 22 ground states. [Iqbal *et al.* (2024), abstract and §§I–III](/reference?id=iqbal2023-nonabelian-topological-order) This experimental result establishes a concrete platform example; it does not demonstrate active error correction or a threshold.

## Relation to symmetry enrichment and ordinary representations

Anyonic fusion, symmetry fractionalization, and ordinary (SU(N)) tensor products are distinct structures. The general categorical setting for non-Abelian anyons and symmetry defects is covered by [Barkeshli *et al.*](/reference?id=barkeshli2014-symmetry-fractionalization). [[concepts/symmetry-enriched-topological-order|Symmetry-enriched topological order]] and [[concepts/lie-algebra-representations|Lie groups, Lie algebras, and representations]] retain the corresponding distinctions.

## SU(2)-symmetric spin-liquid example

An exactly solvable decorated-honeycomb spin model supplies a separate condensed-matter example: after breaking
time reversal, its \(\mathbb Z_2\) vortices bind three Majorana zero modes, carry spin \(1/2\), and have
non-Abelian statistics. [Yao and Lee (2011), abstract and pp. 3–4](/reference?id=yao2011-su2-kitaev) The source
therefore supports the physical compatibility of global \(SU(2)\) symmetry with spinful non-Abelian excitations.
It does not establish that a finite-dimensional \(SU(2)\) tensor-product channel is an anyonic fusion channel,
nor that such a channel is decoder-visible.

## Decoherence and quantum dimension

For a specified channel that incoherently proliferates a non-Abelian anyon \(a\), Sala and Verresen map the resulting trajectories to loop models: the loop weight is set by the quantum dimension of the decohering anyon, while the channel rate sets a string-tension-like parameter.  Their Rényi-moment construction gives coupled \(O(N)\) loop models, and their maximal-decoherence analysis relates the fidelity of logically distinct ground states to random loop and spin models. [Sala and Verresen (2025), pp. 1–4](/reference?id=sala2025-decohering-nonabelian-topological-order)

Their quantum-double examples suggest that sufficiently large quantum dimension can prevent loop proliferation even for maximal noise in the stated channel; smaller dimensions can instead admit critical behavior. This is a channel- and model-specific stability result, not a universal non-Abelian error threshold. It therefore motivates deriving a statistical-mechanics model only after fixing the physical noise and observation channel; it does not validate a threshold, a likelihood, or an advantage for this project's representation-resolved herald decoder. [Sala and Verresen (2025), pp. 3–5](/reference?id=sala2025-decohering-nonabelian-topological-order)
